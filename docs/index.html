How it works

From source files to a stage comparison.

Every step is reproducible. The simulator does not invent biological labels or fish temperature measurements.

01 / Acquire

Collect sources

Download official public records and retain original local study files.

02 / Curate

Check relationships

Reconcile counts, group repeated readings, flag conflicts, and preserve lineage.

03 / Configure

Define a journey

Specify hypothetical stages, durations, setpoints, and disruptions.

04 / Simulate

Follow temperature

Model air response and an explicitly assumed product-lag channel.

05 / Compare

Measure exposure

Calculate degree-hours above a selected reference for routes and stages.

Evidence and next steps

What the results can support.

Engineering checks show that the code reproduces its configured scenarios. Biological prediction requires a separate empirical study.

Supported today

Compare thermal exposure

  • 48 saved runs across two configured routes, three source modes and eight scenarios.
  • The configured air road failure adds 55.8 °C·h above 2 °C, the largest single effect in that scenario set.
  • Road stays largest in 197 of 200 bounded air assumption draws at 2 °C. This is sensitivity, not accuracy.
  • A working local Streamlit dashboard can run and export new configurations.
Blocked by missing observations

Predict actual fish spoilage

  • No matched fish sensor and quality labels for a supervised freshness classifier.
  • No fresh-finfish quality trajectories at multiple measured product temperatures.
  • No independent Indian shipments with paired logger and outcome records.
  • Therefore no calibrated spoilage probability, shelf-life error or field reliability claim.

The next research step is to collect specimen/lot-linked fish temperature and quality measurements, fit species-specific kinetics, and test on independent shipments. A lower degree-hour total cannot by itself establish food safety or a viable voyage.
